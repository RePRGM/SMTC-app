How to loop a song to practice dancing

Replaying a whole song just to drill four bars is the slowest way to practice. Loop the exact part that trips you up and you will learn it in a fraction of the reps.

A loop is just a section set to repeat. Drill it on a tight cycle until the moves are automatic.

By Show Me The Counts · Updated June 2026 · 7 min read

There is always one part. The transition you fumble every time, the fast footwork that falls apart, the eight-count that refuses to stick. And the usual way of practicing it is brutally inefficient: play the song, wait for the part, attempt it once, then rewind and wait again. You spend more time waiting than dancing.

Looping fixes that. Instead of riding the whole track, you set a short section to repeat and drill it over and over with no dead time. It is the single highest-leverage practice habit a dancer can build, and this guide covers what to loop, how to set good loop points, and how to actually practice a loop so it pays off.

why it worksReps where they count

Learning a movement is about repetition, but not just any repetition. Your body needs many focused attempts at the specific thing it cannot do yet, close together, so the pattern moves into muscle memory. Playing a four-minute song to get six attempts at one transition is the opposite of that.

A loop concentrates your practice. Twenty clean reps of the hard eight-count in two minutes will do more for you than running the full song five times. It also keeps your head in the work, because you are not constantly resetting your attention every time you rewind. Break the routine into small pieces, loop the piece, and the difficult part stops being difficult.

Loop the hard part and you trade waiting for reps.

what to loopPick the right section

A loop should be aimed at a specific problem. These are the five most useful things to put on repeat:

  1. 1The eight-count you keep forgetting. Loop a single count until the sequence is locked, then move to the next one.
  2. 2The tricky transition. The seam between two sections is where most routines fall apart. Loop the last beats of one part and the first beats of the next so the join becomes seamless.
  3. 3The fast footwork or shine. Loop it slow, get it clean, then bring the speed up. This is where looping plus slowing down is unbeatable.
  4. 4A musical hit or break. If you are choreographing to a specific accent, loop that moment until your body hits it on instinct.
  5. 5The intro. Loop the first phrase to drill starting on time and finding the 1, the thing that decides whether the whole dance starts right.

Loop the problem, not the song.

how to loopSet good loop points

A sloppy loop fights you. A good one feels like the music was made to repeat. The single most important rule: start and end your loop on the music, not in the middle of it. Begin the loop on a downbeat (a 1 or a 5) and end it right before the next downbeat, so when the loop snaps back it lands cleanly on a beat instead of jarring you off time.

From there, a few habits make loops far more effective:

Loop small, then grow. Master one eight-count on its own before you loop two together, and only then loop the full phrase. Building up in chunks is faster than wrestling the whole thing at once.

Slow the loop down. Drop the tempo until you can do the section perfectly, then nudge the speed up a little at a time. Looping and slowing down are a pair; each makes the other better.

Use a count-in. Catching the 1 on every pass trains you to enter on time, which is exactly the skill you need when the music comes back around in a real song.

Then put it back. Once a loop is clean, stop looping it in isolation and loop the part just before and after it, so the section flows in context. A move you can only do inside its own loop is not finished yet.

Start on a downbeat, loop small, slow it down, then re-integrate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I loop part of a song to practice dancing?

Mark the start and end of the section you want to drill and set it to repeat. A dance practice app like SMTC lets you save that section as a Moment, loop it on repeat, slow it down, and even isolate the instruments, so you can drill one passage instead of replaying the whole song.

What part of a song should I loop?

Loop the specific part that trips you up: a single eight-count you keep forgetting, a tricky transition between two sections, a fast footwork passage or shine, a musical hit you are choreographing to, or the intro if you are working on starting on the 1.

How long should a dance practice loop be?

As short as the problem. Often a single eight-count, or even a four-beat transition. Keep loops on musical boundaries so they start on a downbeat (a 1 or a 5), which keeps the loop seamless and your timing intact.

Does looping help you learn choreography faster?

Yes. Focused repetition of the exact part you struggle with builds muscle memory far faster than replaying a whole song to reach the same few bars each time. Looping removes the dead time and concentrates your reps where they count.

Can I slow down a looped section?

Yes. The most effective drill is to loop a section and slow it down until you can do it cleanly, then raise the tempo back toward full speed a little at a time as it locks into muscle memory.
